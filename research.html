RESEARCH METHOD

Research is a process, not a claim of authority.

Four working categories

Facts

Claims supported by an identifiable source, measurement, record or other evidence.

Observations

Recorded experiences, measurements or project outcomes that may require replication or contextualisation.

Hypotheses

Propositions advanced for testing, including the ASU intergenerational theory.

Opinions

Interpretations or judgements that are labelled as such rather than presented as established facts.

Method

  1. Define the question.
  2. Separate assumptions from evidence.
  3. Search primary, official and academic sources before relying on summaries.
  4. Record alternative explanations and weaknesses.
  5. State what evidence would support or falsify the proposition.
  6. Preserve the version reviewed.
  7. Revise openly when evidence changes the conclusion.

Current research themes

Examples include intergenerational transmission and social development, communication and standards, responsible technical development, policy design, risk governance, and the interaction between individual behaviour and wider systems.
