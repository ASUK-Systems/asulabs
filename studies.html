STUDIES & EVIDENCE BASE

Existing research that informs the work.

This page does not claim that the cited literature proves the ASU theories. It identifies research areas that make the questions scientifically or policy-relevant and shows where the current evidence is stronger, weaker or incomplete.

Intergenerational transmission

The Intergenerational Transfer of Psychosocial Risk

Serbin & Karp review longitudinal evidence on intergenerational continuity, parenting and environmental mechanisms, including factors that can increase risk and factors that can buffer it.

Annual Review of Psychology — review article

Socialization Processes in the Family

Grusec reviews how children learn moral values and social conventions through family socialization and notes complex interactions among biological, social and cultural factors.

Annual Review of Psychology — review article

Comparative Intergenerational Stratification Research

Ganzeboom, Treiman & Ultee review decades of cross-national work on intergenerational transmission of socioeconomic advantage and the measurement challenges involved.

Annual Review of Sociology — review article

Criminal justice and life-course questions

Intergenerational Transmission of Criminal Justice Contact

Wildeman provides a critical overview of research on intergenerational patterns in criminal-justice contact, illustrating why family-level patterns should not automatically be treated as simple inherited traits.

Annual Review of Criminology — review

Desistance as an Intergenerational Process

A later review considers how crime and desistance can be examined across individuals, families and generations, including measurement and mechanisms.

Annual Review of Criminology — 2024 review

Policy and responsible implementation

UK risk management framework

HSE guidance provides a practical model for identifying hazards, assessing risk, controlling risk, recording findings and reviewing controls. This is used as a governance analogy for higher-risk practical work.
