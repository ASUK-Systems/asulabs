RESEARCH RECORD · ASU-IGC-001

Intergenerational Goal Continuity & Social Evolution

A developing framework for examining how individual development, inherited social environments, historical conditions and technological change may interact across generations.

Status: Working hypothesis Revision: 2026.09 First formulation: 12 July 2026 Record type: Independent research

01 · PROPOSITION

Core proposition

Human development does not occur in isolation. Individuals develop within social, material, cultural and institutional environments that contain inherited expectations, knowledge, relationships, constraints and opportunities.

This research asks whether some goals, priorities, fears, values and unresolved conflicts can persist across generations through social and developmental mechanisms, while being altered by changing historical conditions.

The central claim is deliberately narrower than a claim of direct biological inheritance: intergenerational continuity may emerge when inherited social influences are repeatedly reproduced, interpreted and adapted within changing environments.

02 · MECHANISM

Proposed mechanism

Environmental structure

Family, class, institutions, geography, technology, education, economic conditions and social networks shape the environment in which development occurs.

Challenge & uncertainty

Scarcity, conflict, opportunity, instability and changing social expectations can alter which behaviours, skills and priorities are useful or reinforced.

Cognitive & behavioural response

Individuals interpret their environment and respond through learning, decision-making, imitation, avoidance, cooperation and adaptation.

Intergenerational continuity

Some learned priorities and behavioural patterns may influence the next generation through upbringing, communication, expectations, resources and social networks.

03 · BOUNDARY

What this theory does — and does not — claim

Within scope

  • Social and developmental transmission between generations.
  • Interaction between inherited social conditions and historical change.
  • Variation between families, groups and individuals.
  • Mechanisms that could interrupt or redirect continuity.
  • Long-term consequences for behaviour, institutions and culture.

Outside the present evidence claim

  • A claim that acquired goals are genetically inherited.
  • A claim that one historical event directly caused human biological evolution.
  • A prediction that technological change must produce a particular physical human trait.
  • A claim that the framework is an established scientific theory.

04 · RESEARCH QUESTIONS

Questions for investigation

  1. Which forms of goals, values, expectations or behavioural strategies show measurable continuity across generations?
  2. Which transmission pathways are most important: direct teaching, modelling, language, material resources, institutions, social networks or other mechanisms?
  3. How does historical disruption affect continuity?
  4. When do inherited priorities conflict with the environment experienced by the next generation?
  5. Can comparable patterns be identified across different populations and historical periods?
  6. What observations would distinguish intergenerational continuity from ordinary individual learning or shared environmental exposure?

05 · HISTORICAL INVESTIGATION

Information environments, conflict & social change

Historical periods involving war, economic disruption, mass communication, rationing, institutional messaging and major changes in social organisation provide potential comparative cases for examining the framework.

The research question is not whether media control or wartime policy “caused” human evolution. Instead, the question is whether prolonged changes in information, incentives, social roles and material conditions altered behaviour and expectations, and whether any such changes persisted through intergenerational transmission.

Evidence requirement: historical interpretation should be based on dated primary records, demographic evidence, contemporaneous accounts and appropriate comparative cases rather than retrospective assumptions.

06 · TECHNOLOGY

Artificial intelligence as a new cognitive environment

Artificial intelligence introduces a rapidly changing external cognitive environment. Systems can increasingly perform tasks involving information retrieval, language, reasoning, planning and other forms of cognitive work.

A near-term research question is behavioural and cultural: whether repeated AI use changes cognitive offloading, skill acquisition, occupational organisation, learning strategies or patterns of knowledge transmission.

A much longer-term evolutionary question is more speculative: if technological environments create persistent differences in behaviour or reproductive success, could those pressures eventually interact with biological evolution? Any such claim would require heritable variation, differential reproductive consequences and persistence over many generations. Specific predictions about future human anatomy are therefore not made by this record.

07 · SCIENTIFIC CONTEXT

Related evidence domains

Gene–culture interaction

Research in gene–culture coevolution examines how cultural practices can alter environments and selection pressures, while genetic and cultural inheritance operate through different mechanisms.

Human brain evolution

Modern genomic research can investigate molecular and cellular mechanisms relevant to human brain evolution, but many links between genomic variation, development, cognition and long-term evolutionary change remain unresolved.

Cultural transmission

Cultural evolution research provides established mechanisms for studying socially transmitted behaviour, learning, imitation, norms and cumulative change.

Cognitive offloading

Recent research on AI-assisted cognition raises questions about how external systems affect learning and skill retention. Effects appear dependent on how the technology is used rather than being uniformly beneficial or harmful.

This section identifies evidence domains for further study. It does not treat related literature as proof of the present hypothesis.

08 · CRITICAL TEST

What would strengthen or weaken the framework?

Potentially supporting evidence

  • Replicated longitudinal evidence of specific priorities or strategies persisting across generations after relevant environmental factors are controlled.
  • Evidence identifying measurable transmission pathways.
  • Cross-cultural or historical replication with appropriate comparison groups.
  • Evidence showing that disruption predicts measurable changes in continuity.

Potentially weakening evidence

  • Failure to reproduce intergenerational patterns under controlled or well-designed comparative studies.
  • Evidence showing that apparent continuity is fully explained by shared environment or selection effects.
  • Findings that proposed mechanisms do not predict observed outcomes.
  • Strong competing explanations that account for the observations with fewer assumptions.

10 · STATUS

Research status & interpretation rule

WORKING HYPOTHESIS

This is an independent developing hypothesis. It is not presented as an established scientific conclusion, and publication on this site does not establish scientific validity.

Evidence cited by ASU Labs supports the specific source or research domain from which it originates. It should not be interpreted automatically as evidence that the complete ASU framework is correct.

Future revisions should preserve the distinction between established evidence, supported interpretation, working hypothesis, speculative hypothesis and open research question.
